# Library triage — which systems are worth reading

Desk research over the 112 systems in [library.md](library.md), to decide what to read
next without opening 3,841 PDFs. Judged on fit with Wyrd's **grim, low-fantasy, single-PC,
text-only** brief. Sourced from published reviews and publisher material, not from the PDFs
themselves — so treat as a reading order, not a verdict.

## Tier A — read next

### Dragon Age / AGE system (33 PDFs)
**The single most relevant unread system in the library, for mechanical reasons.**

AGE resolves every test as **3d6 + ability vs a target number**, with one of the three dice
— the **Dragon Die** — a different colour. If any two dice show the same number (doubles)
and the test succeeds, the character generates **stunt points equal to the Dragon Die**.

That is almost exactly the dice architecture proposed for Wyrd, published in 2009 and
playtested for fifteen years. Critically, **the doubles trigger is orthogonal to the
total** — which is the property that makes independent success/side-effect axes possible.
See [dice-design.md](dice-design.md). Doubles occur ~44% of the time, so it is frequent
enough to matter and rare enough to stay special.

Read: core rulebook for the stunt lists (combat, spell, roleplaying, exploration stunts) —
they are effectively a pre-written boon table.

### Crowns 2e (10 PDFs)
Action-horror fantasy "set in a dark world where humanity lost the war against evil and are
now reduced to scattered refuges in a vast, ruin-haunted wilderness." Advertises a **fear
system**, travel and camp mechanics, dungeon procedures, strongholds and warfare, and
"bloody-grueling combat... bountiful amounts of death and dismemberment." There is a
**Black Powder** expansion for gunpowder-age play.

Tonally this is the closest non-Warhammer match found: a losing war, scattered refuges,
fear as a subsystem, and guns. Read for the fear system and the stronghold rules
(which pair with TOR's Holdings).

### Beyond the Wall — *Further Afield* (77 PDFs)
Core game is village-scale zero-prep OSR with collaborative character generation. The
supplement is the interesting part: **Threat Packs** — "a bit like large-scale Scenario
Packs, except that they dictate the course of events over the length of the campaign,
rather than setting up a single adventure." Four worked examples (a dragon-boss, an
imperial invasion, a magic kidnapper, a creeping blight), plus procedural generation of the
village and then the surrounding region.

**This is a procedural meta-campaign engine** — the generative counterpart to TOR's
authored Tale of Years. For Wyrd, which must *select and sequence* rather than follow a
script, Threat Packs may be the better model. High priority.

### BareBones Fantasy (37 PDFs)
84-page complete system, roll-under percentile, doubles as crits/fumbles. The reason to
read it: it ships **adventure, dungeon and reward generators**, and reviewers specifically
call it "a good candidate for solitary play." Read the generators, not the system.

## Tier B — mine for content, not rules

### White Dwarf (218), Warpstone (20), Strike to Stun, Liber Fanatica
Already flagged in [scenarios.md](scenarios.md). Still the richest short-form seam.

### Mordheim (114) + Coreheim (4) + Wyrdwars (26)
Mordheim is the cursed-city setting and its **serious injury table** (D66) is a
better-developed version of exactly what Warlock's criticals do. **Coreheim** is a fan
rebalance — noted by critics as removing randomness and treating itself as "a 'serious'
wargame rather than something that creates glorious stories," so it is the *less* useful
version for Wyrd's purposes. **Wyrdwars** is another Mordheim variant with 23 warband
files — useful as faction/NPC-group source material.

Read Mordheim for injuries, warband upkeep between games, and the city itself.

### Crimson Blades (2 PDFs)
Old-school dark fantasy / sword & sorcery, described as "an alternative universe style
Solomon Kane world," with rules for **summoning and binding demons, elementals and
undead**, and an anti-quasi-medieval, orientalist pulp aesthetic. Open content under OGL.
Worth a look for the demon-binding rules; the setting is a poor fit.

### Maelstrom (17)
Already flagged. Grim historical English fantasy, needs renaming rather than conversion.

### The tone donors
Dark Future, Wreck Age, Atomic Highway, Exodus, Mutant Chronicles, Deadlands, Weird West,
The End of the World — as previously assessed, structurally reskinnable to 40k hive or
Old World horror.

## Tier C — specific narrow uses

- **Mythic (2)** — the GM Emulator. Already identified; the oracle discipline that stops
  solo play becoming wish-fulfilment.
- **Scarlet Heroes (4)** — explicitly built for **one PC**, with damage scaling so a single
  character survives party-scale content. Directly relevant to Wyrd's core constraint.
- **Cairn (13) / Mausritter (133) / Troika! (4)** — Into the Odd / FF lineage. Rules-light,
  strong situation design, good reaction and encounter tables.
- **Sellswords and Spellslingers (11), Rangers of Shadow Deep (3), Frostgrave (7)** — solo
  and co-op skirmish with AI-driven opponents. Read for **how they run NPC companions and
  enemy behaviour without a GM** — directly applicable to Wyrd's NPC party.
- **OnePage (341 PDFs)** — one-page adventures/dungeons. Enormous quantity of
  session-sized, system-agnostic situations. Worth indexing even if quality varies.
- **Adventurers! (77)** — rules-light fantasy, players' guide fits on two pages. Originally
  Polish. Useful as a compactness benchmark, not a source.
- **QUERP (10)** — "Quick Easy Role Playing", `2d6 + stat vs difficulty`, six stats
  (Fighting, Magic, Strength, Knowledge, Reflexes, Stealth). Aimed at beginners. Low value
  — Warlock does this better.

## Tier D — not relevant to Wyrd

Checked and set aside, so they don't get re-examined later:

- **Red Sand Black Moon** — fantasy *gladiatorial miniatures skirmish* (Two Hour Wargames),
  not an RPG. Arena combat only.
- **Folklore Realms** — 18th-century folk-horror cooperative storytelling game, requires a
  Storymaster plus 2-4 players. Tonally interesting, structurally group-dependent.
- **Storymasters Tales** — same designer (Oliver McNeil); little published detail found.
  Low priority.
- **Pathfinder (604)**, **D&D (39)**, **Dungeon World (56)**, **Savage Worlds (81)**,
  **Microlite20 (49)**, **Tiny D6 (42)**, **Hero Kids (40)** — high fantasy or generic;
  wrong register. Pathfinder's 604 PDFs are the library's largest block and the least
  useful to Wyrd.
- **Descent, Space Hulk, Blood Bowl, Diskwars, Kings of War, Dragon Rampant, Brikwars,
  Songs of Blades, Mini Gangs, Rogue Stars, Bag of Dungeon, Darkfast Dungeons** — board and
  miniatures games. Setting colour only.
- **Elite Dangerous, Star Wars, Traveller, Star Frontiers, Stars Without Number, Covert
  Ops** — sci-fi, but not grim in the 40k sense. Hold for the sci-fi conversion; Stars
  Without Number's sector generators may earn a look then.

## Reading order

1. **Dragon Age** core — stunt mechanics and stunt tables (dice design)
2. **Beyond the Wall: Further Afield** — Threat Packs (meta-campaign)
3. **Crowns 2e** — fear system, strongholds (subsystems)
4. **Mordheim** — injury table, warband upkeep (grit)
5. **Scarlet Heroes** — single-PC scaling (core constraint)
6. **Sellswords / Rangers of Shadow Deep** — NPC companion and enemy AI
7. **BareBones Fantasy** — adventure/dungeon generators
